# Overall thoughts

The structure of this is excellent. The core mechanic - Fabrication and what that means for human bodies - is front and center immediately in the story. You gradually gain little affordances at regular intervals. You get used to the mechanic and then find a way to improve it or broaden your scope. Each little section gave you a little bit of story AND a little bit of mechanics. Overall the game starts with strong story, minimal mechanics, but then opens up to primarily mechanics with the story skulking in the background, then brings it home with strong story and meaningful choices.

The ship isn't too huge but is a big enough space to explore. Fast travel was exceptionally useful and not curmudgeoningly frowned upon by the game. Survival elements kept you moving, and the subtle introduction of items when they were needed was great.

The story itself had a strong backbone of theme (Ship of Theseus), and while not an expansive story, it was dense with twists. The plot integrated with the mechanics and the theme. The characters were well-written. Some of the jokey humour almost strained the mood, but it ended up reading like goofy humour to lighten the mood in a horrific situation.

The sound and graphic design were great. My only gripe was the KATHUNK of the recycler instead of something dismantling an object atom-by-atom.

I could see some very tiny rooms for improvement: icons to help distinguish the resources, and maybe a tighter way to reveal resource info instead of expanding sections. Oh and once you'd vacuumed up a room, most remained empty and not useful.

The choices were both explicit - which of these three do you choose? - but also implicit - do I turn GAIL on?
