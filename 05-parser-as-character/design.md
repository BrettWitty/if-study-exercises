# Design exercise in "Parser as Character"

## Exercise

In drawing, the negative space is the space around the focus of the picture and is just as important as the focus. Design a scene with a negative space character — one in which they never appear, but their impact on the scene is almost as if they were there. 

## Answer

The scene is a round-table discussion at a long table in a dark room. People sit on either side and at the very end, a large, imposing chair sits empty at the end. The people either side are the second tier in the hierarchy of a cult. The leader has been taken by the Feds and they squabble about what to do. One of them is paranoid that this very discussion is being monitored by the Feds, so they talk obliquely but desperately.

One man demands they go rescue him, by any means necessary. He's even happy to arm up and storm the cops.

A woman is suggesting cooler heads might prevail. She knows the commune needs to survive this, with or without The Leader.

Another woman falls into growing paranoia.

A man tries to discern what The Leader might suggest, but clearly is having trouble thinking for himself for the first time in ages. He keeps reading passages from the Holy Text, hoping to contextualise the situation and the discussion in a way he knows how. He thinks he's helping the others, but is mostly confusing them and abrogating his own responsibility to input useful thoughts.

At appropriate beats, the player selects who speaks next, which sets the dynamics for the scene.
