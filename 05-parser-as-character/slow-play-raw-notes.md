# Slow play notes for *Violet*

## Tasks

1. Log turns, noting when a response is in Violet's voice or the Inform 7 parser.
2. Push boundaries and note responses.
3. Track what you learn about Violet.

## Notes

- Sense of appropriateness: I was supposed to have this done a while ago. I'm on holiday so I have time, but I procrastinated for an hour or so.
- Immediately the parser voice is Violet.
- Details:
  - It's a Saturday.
  - It's my 30th birthday
  - Violet is upset.
- Explicit "this is what you can do":
  > WRITE
  > EXAMINE DESK (or X DESK)
  > TAKE ITINERARY
  > OPEN DRAWER
  > STAND ON CHAIR
  > LOOK UNDER DESK
  > PUT ITINERARY ON CABINET
- The about is in her voice.
- There are hints.
- Interesting point about default maleness. In a way this makes the PC a blank slate, and their persona is less fundamental to the story, although Violet isn't.
- Call to action is explicitly "WRITE", multiple times. It offers the first puzzle (SIT DOWN)
  - Feels a bit like the Babelfish puzzle. Just write! WRITE. Just sit down first. SIT DOWN. Turn on your word processor! OPEN WORD PROCESSOR. Hmm, a tattoo.
- TYVTWD: Take Your Violet To Work Day. Part of the pretence is a life hack.
- "You've made some really handsome margin and font choices already for your dissertation, budgie"
- A lot of nice hints on what to do next, laced with slight bitterness of the past. Julia comes off real bad, although that's maybe expected via Violet.
- The Australianisms are very much Australianisms an American would come up with. Maybe inauthenticity works because of the Imaginary Violet.
- The imaginary sword is a funny subversion. Violet constantly jabbing at you is neat.
- Funny how everything has a violet tinge: lilac, fuschia, primrose...
- It acknowledged EAT KEY!
- The puzzles are getting more obtuse now. I like the detailed interactions, but gum in my ears, headphones on, clip on my nose... Jeepers.
- I finished it, with some glances at the author's walkthrough.
  - I liked it, although I was hoping for a slightly different game, tonally. It still worked, but there was a friction with the seriousness of the relationship and the ludicrousness of the situation. The simulation was excellent and smooth. I liked the progressive reveal of complexity (especially with the crazy platyPod).
